Choose the method before the gear

Three questions to find gear you will actually use

No brewer suits everyone. Match your taste, routine, and main priority first, then see the trade-offs and minimum setup. This is not a brand ranking and contains no affiliate shopping links.

Build the picture first

Never seen the gear? Start with the shape

There is no need to memorise names. Notice the silhouette, where it sits, and what moves during brewing so you can recognise the real object immediately.

01

V60 pour-over

“V” refers to the V-shaped cone; “60” refers to its 60-degree cone angle.

How to recognise it
It looks like an upside-down triangular funnel with a handle and base. It sits on a mug or server and holds a cone paper filter.
What you actually do
Pour hot water from above and coffee drips into the vessel below. You control where and how quickly the water lands.

02

AeroPress

AeroPress is the brand and product name, not an angle or size.

How to recognise it
Look for two wide tubes fitted together: a plunger on top and a flanged chamber that sits directly on a sturdy mug.
What you actually do
Coffee steeps inside the chamber, then you press down so the brew passes through the filter into the mug.
Common mix-up
It is not a French press: AeroPress pushes brewed coffee through a filter into another cup; a French press keeps the brew in its pot and pushes the grounds down.

03

French press

The name describes the French-press family of immersion pots with a plunger and mesh filter.

How to recognise it
It resembles a glass or metal teapot with a vertical rod; a round metal mesh filter sits inside the pot.
What you actually do
Grounds and water steep in the same pot. When time is up, lower the mesh slowly and pour the coffee out.

04

Cold-brew pot / covered vessel

“Cold brew” is a method, not one brand or one required shape.

How to recognise it
A dedicated brewer often resembles a juice pitcher with a long mesh basket hanging inside. A covered vessel and filter bag also work.
What you actually do
Coffee and cold water steep for hours in the fridge, then you remove the basket or filter the brew.

A purchase order that avoids waste

  1. 1Choose the method you will really use each week.
  2. 2A normal kettle can get you started; upgrade to a gooseneck only when pouring becomes the V60 bottleneck.
  3. 3A scale improves repeatability; a burr grinder usually affects consistency more directly than decorative accessories.
  4. 4After 5–10 brews, use your records to decide whether temperature control, a server, or extra filters solve a real problem.
