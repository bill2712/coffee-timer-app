Frequently asked questions about coffee beans

What is the "degassing" process of coffee beans?
During the roasting process, coffee beans produce large amounts of carbon dioxide that is locked within the cell structure. Freshly roasted coffee beans are very outgassing. If brewed immediately, a large number of bubbles will prevent the contact between hot water and coffee powder, resulting in insufficient extraction and weak flavor (that is, the common water channel effect). The so-called "bean raising" is a physical process in which coffee beans are left to rest for several days to allow excess carbon dioxide to be naturally released and to achieve the best balance of coffee flavor.
How to properly store coffee beans to maintain freshness?
The three enemies of preserving coffee beans are: oxygen, light and moisture. The best way to store coffee beans is to place them in a sealed bag with a one-way exhaust valve or an opaque vacuum jar in a cool, dry cupboard without direct sunlight. Never put coffee beans that are used frequently in the refrigerator into the refrigerator, because the moisture generated by the temperature difference when opening and closing the refrigerator will cause the coffee beans to get damp quickly and absorb the odor in the refrigerator.
Can I freeze coffee beans in the refrigerator?
Yes, but there are strict conditions. If you buy a large amount of coffee beans that you can't finish in a month, you can "portion" them into small single-brew portions (such as centrifuge tubes or vacuum bags) and place them deep in the freezer. When you want to brew, just take out the required amount "no need to remove the ice" and grind it immediately. Be careful not to repeatedly put the entire bag of beans in and out of the freezer, as the condensed moisture will completely destroy the flavor of the entire bag of coffee.
