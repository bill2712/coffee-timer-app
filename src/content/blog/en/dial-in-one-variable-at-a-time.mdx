## Separate extraction from strength

A weak cup is not automatically under-extracted; it may simply contain too much water for the coffee dose. Likewise, an intense cup is not necessarily well extracted. Before changing a recipe, ask two separate questions: does the cup taste sharp and hollow, balanced, or dry and harsh? Is the body too light, appropriate, or too concentrated?

The Barista Hustle Coffee Compass separates adjustments that change extraction from adjustments that change strength. It is a useful starting framework, but the bean, roast, water, and uneven flow can create similar sensations. Treat every diagnosis as a hypothesis to test in the next cup.

## A controlled adjustment loop

1. Keep the coffee, dose, water, temperature, and pouring pattern fixed.
2. Change the grind by one small step. A sharp, thin cup may benefit from a finer grind; a dry cup with a slow finish may benefit from a coarser grind.
3. Taste each cup at a similar temperature and record acidity, bitterness, sweetness, body, and finish.
4. Repeat a setting at least once. Large differences between identical recipes usually point to inconsistent pouring or preparation.
5. Only after the grind direction is clear should you test ratio or temperature.

## Avoid common misdiagnoses

Acidity is not automatically a defect. Clear fruit-like acidity supported by sweetness differs from a sharp, short, sometimes salty sensation. Bitterness may also come from roast style rather than brew time alone. If one cup is simultaneously sharp and drying, inspect uneven flow, excessive agitation, or filter clogging before chasing a target time.

## Keep an actionable record

Five fields are enough for a useful log: grind setting, ratio, water temperature, total time, and overall score. Add one sentence stating what you will change next. Barista Flow's brew history and A/B comparison are designed around this single-variable workflow.
