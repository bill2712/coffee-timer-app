## Rinsing does more than address paper flavor

Hot-water rinsing can remove loose paper fibers or noticeable paper flavor, preheat the dripper and server, and help the filter sit against the brewer. HARIO's published V60 technique includes rinsing the paper with hot water as a preparation step.

Rinsing does not solve every flow problem. Paper material, thickness, folds, and fit can all affect drainage. When comparing filters, keep grind and pouring fixed rather than changing the recipe as soon as total time differs.

## Record three flow checkpoints

A single “finished at 3:30” number hides useful information. Record the end of the bloom, the end of the final pour, and the end of drawdown. A brew that flows normally at first and slows sharply near the end suggests fines migration or filter clogging. A brew that is slow from the first pour may point to an overly fine grind, a blocked outlet, or too much coffee for the brewer.

## A troubleshooting order

1. On the next brew, remove one swirl or stir while keeping everything else unchanged.
2. If the finish remains unusually slow, move one small step coarser.
3. Check whether the water stream repeatedly strikes one area and moves fines toward the paper.
4. Compare filter papers side by side, recording both flow and flavor rather than time alone.
5. Confirm that folding or vacuum contact has not sealed the brewer's drainage path.

Coffee ad Astra explains that excessive agitation can lodge fines in paper pores, leaving only a few active flow paths. The long time is then a symptom of uneven flow. Conversely, a longer brew without clogging is not automatically bad; taste remains the final test.

## A simple comparison

Brew two cups with the same recipe. Swirl the first only once and swirl the second after every pour. Record the time from the final pour to the end of drawdown and compare dryness. If the second cup finishes much later and tastes drier, reduce agitation before changing filter, grind, and temperature together.
